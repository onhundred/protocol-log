# Voice: Onhundred

How Onhundred writes: who speaks, how it sounds, and the rules every page follows. It sits next to DESIGN.md (how it looks) and PRODUCT.md (what it is).

Last updated: Oct 12, 2026.

## North star: the deadpan field log

A lab that takes your life far too seriously, written by the person it is happening to. The method is calm, exact and procedural. The person running it is candid, specific and a little uncomfortable. The writing works when the two meet: a clinical rule applied, with a straight face, to something deeply human.

**References**
- **Shape Up** (Basecamp): a method that reads like a story. Plain names kept consistently, every rule backed by a reason, short chapters, real examples as evidence.
- **Nathan Fielder's deadpan:** complete procedural seriousness applied to intimate or absurd situations. Nobody winks. The humor comes from the commitment, not from jokes.

## Who speaks

Onhundred has two voices, mirroring DESIGN.md's Two Voices Rule (Archivo for the subject, Martian Mono for the lab).

| Voice | Who | Sounds like | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The lab | The method | Short, exact, imperative or impersonal. No adjectives it can't defend | Rules, steps, tables, cards, labels, parameters |
| The subject | Ale, first person | Candid, specific, doubtful when it should be. Admits cost | Forewords, step openers, field notes, experiment posts, the site |

### Where each voice appears

| Place | Lab | Subject |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Protocol | Everywhere | A one-page foreword only ("Why I wrote this") |
| Getting started guide | Instructions, cards, prompts | A short field note opening each step, 3 sentences at most |
| Experiment posts and pages | The experiment card | Everything around it |
| Site | Labels, statuses, grid | Manifesto, confession lines, experiment questions |
| Company-facing material | Everywhere, in a formal register | Never |

**Why this split:** the protocol has to stay portable, so a company can adopt it without adopting a person. The guide's readers need "someone like me" before they act, so they need the person. The rules stay in one voice so nobody confuses a story with a rule.

**Field notes are real or absent.** A field note reports something that happened in the reference application. Until it happens, the opener is a reason or an admission, never an invented anecdote.

## Tone dials

| Dial | Setting |
| --- | --- |
| Formal to casual | Middle. Contractions yes; slang, filler and internet speak no |
| Serious to funny | Serious on the surface, funny underneath. Deadpan only |
| Warm to cold | Warm about people, cold about the method |
| Certain to doubtful | Certain about rules, honest about results we don't have yet |
| Hype | None |

## Humor: deadpan only

- **Where it comes from:** procedure applied to personal life with total seriousness. A risk score for dinner. A success test for a birthday message. Never from jokes, puns, emojis or exclamation marks.
- **Never wink.** No "yes, really," no "lol," no "spoiler." The reader decides it's funny.
- **The target is the subject or the method.** Never the partner, the children, family, employees, students, buyers or anyone else involved.
- **No humor in:** the Warning page, consent, children, health, stop criteria, data handling, legal text and anything company-facing.
- **One per page at most.** If every line is a bit, nothing is.

**Examples of the register (illustrations, not real data)**
- "P-HOM-03 · Get tonight's dinner on the table. Frequency: 5. Error risk: 4, according to my family."
- "EXP-03 asks whether an agent can book our date night restaurant. The bet: it will choose better than I do, because I always choose the same one."
- "The holding period is 3 weeks. Not because 3 is scientific. Because 2 felt like luck and 4 felt like bureaucracy."

## Storytelling rules

1. **Open with the stake, then the rule.** Say what something costs before saying what to do about it.
2. **One protagonist.** The reference application is the running example. Real numbers, real choices, real failures, once they exist.
3. **Concrete over abstract.** "Answer the school group chat," not "relationship logistics." Name the thing before the code.
4. **Every unit has tension.** Every experiment has a bet that can lose. Sections end on what's at risk or what comes next, not on a summary.
5. **Every rule has a reason,** in one sentence, close to the rule.
6. **Rhythm.** Mostly short sentences. A longer one when it earns it. An occasional one-line paragraph for the turn.
7. **Honesty is the hook.** Admit cost, doubt and failure. Wrong bets get the same space as right ones.
8. **A jargon budget.** Each protocol word is introduced once, with a plain explanation, and never changes after.

## Sentence rules

- No em-dashes. Use a period, a comma or a colon.
- Second person, active voice, for instructions ("You record," not "It is recorded").
- First person singular for the subject. "I," not "we," except in company-facing text.
- Results first: lead with what the reader gets or what happened.
- No unverified figures. Every number is measured, sourced or labeled as an estimate.
- Contractions are fine. Exclamation marks are not.
- Numbers as digits (3 weeks, 100 loops).
- Aim for sentences under 25 words.
- No swearing, ever.

**Use, and keep consistent**

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Loop | One recurring responsibility. It keeps coming back until someone closes it (never "part," "task" or "job"). Named by its output, never by a decision (see LOOPS.md) |
| Cycle | The two-week rhythm of Map, Delegate, Review. Never call a cycle a loop, or a loop a cycle |
| Experiment | The story of handing a loop to an agent (never "test") |
| Question | What an experiment answers, in one sentence |
| Bet | The hypothesis, written as If, then, because |
| Stage | One level of an experiment |
| Help me / Do it, I approve / Do it alone | The levels, in plain words (Assisted, Delegated, Autonomous in the protocol) |
| Easy, hard, fun | The three slots |
| Log | The record of everything |
| Owner | The person running the protocol (in the protocol only; the guide says "you") |

**Retired:** part (now loop), subject (now owner, except in the site's Social Experiment 001 framing), test, free slot, Phase 0.

**Never use:** leverage, unlock, empower, revolutionize, game-changer, seamless, cutting-edge, supercharge, journey, delve, robust, "AI-powered," "10x," "hack," "in today's world," "the future of work."

## Templates

**Section opener (guide):** one stake or admission, then the instruction.
> You probably think you know how long email takes you. Measure it once. Before an agent touches a loop, you watch it happen 3 times and write down what it really costs.

**Field note (only when real):** three sentences. What I expected. What happened. What I changed.

**Experiment post:** the question as the title, then the bet, the stages and where each one stands, results against the success tests, and what surprised me. Wrong bets and stopped stages are published the same way.

**Cycle update:** what was delegated, what failed, what's left. One line on what changed in the method.

**Warning:** fixed text, never edited for tone. It appears after the cover of every document.

## Before and after

**Before (lab only):** "A loop's baseline is the measured picture of what it costs today. Without it, nobody can later prove the agent made anything better."
**After:** "You can't prove an agent saved you time if you never knew how much time it took. So you measure first. Three times. Then you're allowed to hope."

**Before:** "Early wins keep a long effort alive, hard loops are where most of the learning is, and a loop you enjoy keeps you engaged."
**After:** "Pick one loop you'll probably win, one you'll probably lose, and one you just want to see happen. The first keeps you going. The second teaches you something. The third is why you'll still be doing this in month three."

## Spanish

- Neutral Latin American Spanish, "tú."
- Deadpan translates; wordplay doesn't. Avoid idioms and puns in English so the Spanish version can keep the joke.
- Keep the protocol's words fixed across languages: loop (kept in English, like "deadline"), experimento, apuesta, etapa, bitácora.

## Company-facing register

Formal, plain and results first. "We" for Onhundred, the company's name for the company. No humor, no first-person stories, no casual phrasing. Results and rules carry the weight.
